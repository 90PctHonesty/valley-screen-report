Where this comes from

Check these yourself

cmiphx.com

Classic Movies In Phoenix. A hand-kept daily calendar of every repertory and anniversary screening in the metro, with ticket links per chain and honest "not on sale yet" flags. Better than anything the chains publish. There is no Tucson equivalent.

advancescreenings.com

Aggregates Gofobo plus the local outlets that actually get Phoenix allocations. Check the Tempe and Scottsdale pages too — most screenings land there, not in Phoenix proper.

redcarpetcrash.com

Their Phoenix category posts passes roughly a day before the aggregators. Worth checking direct. Mind on Movies' own front page is still serving January 2018 archive content, so treat it as unreliable and use its links only through the aggregators.

Harkins email & Facebook

Where both Dune drops were announced first, with no press release and about thirty minutes' notice. The single highest-value thing on this list to subscribe to.

harkins.com/events-and-series

Anime at Harkins, Studio Ghibli, Tuesday Night Classics, Movies In 35MM, Kubrick Spotlight, Secret Cinema. New bookings appear under a series before they appear anywhere else — and the underlying data on any theatre page lists series that have no public page yet, which is how the LOTR series surfaced. The store's Tickets & Vouchers page is where series passes appear.

Pollack Tempe Cinemas

A rotating weekly classics slate, listed through cmiphx.com rather than anywhere reliable of its own. Also polling on Facebook for future classic series — LOTR, the Mummy trilogy, Twilight and Stephen King are on the ballot.

The Loft is not carrying Akira either; its Sep 4 slate is American Pachuco and Big Trouble in Little China.

Watch Crunchyroll's news page for the national theater list. Same two traps as the Valley tab: the leftover 2020 Funimation listings are still live on AMC, Fandango and imax.com, and the July announcement coverage tells readers to reserve tickets as boilerplate. Where this comes from

Check these yourself

loftcinema.org

The single most important source for Tucson, and it does more of the job than any one Phoenix source does. A nonprofit five-screen house with 35mm and 70mm on Screen 1. The Coming Soon page carries dated listings six weeks out with series labels; individual film pages give exact dates, prices and whether showtimes have posted. Series worth tracking: Cult Classics, Mondo Mondays, CINEMA '86, Hollywood Classics, Main Gate Square, Loft Film Fest.

foxtucson.com/events

The 1930 downtown movie palace. Its film programming hides inside a mixed music-and-comedy events calendar under the "Picture Palace" filter, so scan the whole list. Currently the Marilyn Fox Film Series at $10 all-in, and live-score events like Ghostbusters In Concert.

galaxytheatres.com/movie-theater/tucson

Tucson's IMAX screen, with a dedicated /imax showtimes page listing by date — the place any IMAX event booking will surface. Also runs Flashback Cinema and Classic Monday Movies, though the Flashback blog post is often months stale; trust the showtimes, not the blog.

redcarpetcrash.com — Tucson

Has a dedicated Tucson category at /free-movie-screenings/tucson/. It is empty as of today, but it is the same outlet that beats the aggregators to Phoenix passes by a day, so it is the first place a Tucson pass would appear.

advancescreenings.com/city/us/az/tucson

Thin by comparison with Phoenix — currently no screenings and no registered outlets at all. The one named Tucson outlet on the site is 43KIX Tucson, which distributes through Facebook and cannot be read automatically. Expect long empty stretches and do not mistake that for a broken check.

harkins.com/theatres/tucson-spectrum

Harkins' Tucson house — CINÉ XL with Dolby Atmos, no IMAX. Chain-wide series like the Kubrick Spotlight, Tuesday Night Classics and the pending LOTR extended-cut series are booked centrally, so Valley announcements usually mean Tucson dates too. The page renders blank to automated reads; the data behind it does not.

Six IMAX screens exist in the Phoenix metro: Harkins Arizona Mills (the only one in Arizona with 70mm film capability), AMC Mesa Grand, Desert Ridge, Deer Valley, Westgate, and Superstition East. In Tucson, the confirmed IMAX screen is Galaxy Theatres Tucson; the only 70mm-capable screen is the Loft's Screen 1.

Everything above was verified on the date shown. Sold-out status, showtimes and pass availability change fast — confirm before you drive. Tucson is about a hundred miles from Tempe; check twice before making that one.
